# NUVRA — Botanical Sparkling Drinks

## 01. Project Overview

**Brand:** NUVRA  
**Industry:** Non-alcoholic beverages / lifestyle  
**Location:** Urban India  
**Project Type:** Brand Strategy + Visual Identity + Packaging  
**Brand Stage:** New launch  

NUVRA is a modern botanical sparkling drink brand created for people who want a more interesting alternative to traditional soft drinks.

The drinks combine sparkling water with botanical extracts, fruits, herbs, and subtle natural flavours. They are designed to feel sophisticated enough for social occasions while remaining casual enough for everyday consumption.

The founders want NUVRA to sit somewhere between a premium lifestyle beverage and an accessible everyday refreshment.

The brand will initially launch with three flavours:

- **Citrus Basil**
- **Peach Ginger**
- **Berry Hibiscus**

The product will be sold through premium grocery stores, cafés, restaurants, quick-commerce platforms, and the brand's own website.

---

## 02. The Business Problem

The non-alcoholic beverage market is crowded.

Traditional soft drinks often appear:

- overly sweet
- artificial
- mass-market
- visually loud

At the opposite end, many wellness beverages appear:

- overly clinical
- too health-focused
- expensive
- serious
- visually predictable

NUVRA wants to occupy the space between these two categories.

The brand needs to feel:

**refreshing + contemporary + expressive + premium**

without becoming either a traditional soda brand or another minimalist wellness product.

The identity must make the product recognizable on a crowded retail shelf while also looking desirable in cafés, social media photographs, and lifestyle environments.

---

## 03. The Design Challenge

Create a visual identity that makes botanical sparkling drinks feel contemporary, social, and culturally relevant.

The identity should communicate natural ingredients without relying on predictable visual clichés such as leaves, water droplets, green packaging, or generic wellness imagery.

The packaging should become one of the brand's strongest marketing tools.

The goal is to create something consumers would immediately notice, photograph, and remember.

---

## 04. Brand Objective

NUVRA wants to become a recognizable lifestyle beverage for young urban consumers who are reducing their consumption of traditional sugary sodas and alcohol.

The brand should make choosing a non-alcoholic drink feel like a positive lifestyle choice rather than a compromise.

### Primary objectives

- Create a distinctive visual identity.
- Build strong shelf recognition.
- Develop a flexible packaging system for multiple flavours.
- Position NUVRA as contemporary but approachable.
- Create an identity capable of expanding into future products.
- Make the product visually attractive in physical and digital environments.

---

## 05. Target Audience

### Primary Audience

**Age:** 22–35

Urban professionals, designers, creators, founders, students, and young working adults.

They live primarily in metropolitan cities such as:

- Mumbai
- Delhi NCR
- Bengaluru
- Pune
- Hyderabad

They regularly spend money on:

- specialty coffee
- restaurants
- fashion
- fitness
- concerts
- travel
- design-led products
- premium groceries

They discover brands through Instagram, cafés, creators, recommendations, and quick-commerce platforms.

They care about what a product tastes like, but also how it looks and what it represents.

### Secondary Audience

Consumers aged 30–45 who are intentionally reducing alcohol or sugary beverages but still want something sophisticated to drink during dinners, gatherings, or social occasions.

---

## 06. Audience Insight

Consumers increasingly want beverages that fit multiple moments.

They may want something interesting:

- during lunch
- after work
- at a house party
- while working
- with dinner
- during a weekend gathering

The product therefore cannot look exclusively like a health drink.

It must feel equally comfortable next to a laptop, at a restaurant table, or inside a party refrigerator.

---

## 07. Brand Positioning

### Positioning Statement

NUVRA is a modern sparkling botanical drink for people who want refreshment with more character.

It combines unexpected botanical flavours with expressive design to create a sophisticated alternative to ordinary soft drinks.

---

## 08. Brand Idea

### “Refreshment with character.”

NUVRA celebrates flavour combinations that are slightly unexpected.

Citrus becomes Citrus + Basil.  
Peach becomes Peach + Ginger.  
Berry becomes Berry + Hibiscus.

The visual identity should follow the same principle.

Familiar elements should be combined in unexpected ways.

---

## 09. Brand Personality

NUVRA is:

**Expressive** — Never anonymous or generic.  
**Curious** — Interested in unusual ingredients, culture, food, art, and experiences.  
**Confident** — The brand does not need to explain itself excessively.  
**Playful** — Unexpected compositions, language, and visual details are welcome.  
**Sophisticated** — Playfulness should never become childish.

---

## 10. Brand Archetype

### Primary Archetype — The Explorer

NUVRA encourages curiosity, discovery, and experimentation.

### Secondary Archetype — The Creator

The brand values originality, aesthetics, culture, and self-expression.

---

## 11. Brand Values

### Curiosity
Interesting combinations are more memorable than predictable ones.

### Individuality
The brand celebrates personal taste rather than following conventional beverage categories.

### Quality
Ingredients, production, packaging, photography, and communication should feel considered.

### Sociability
NUVRA exists naturally within conversations, gatherings, meals, and shared experiences.

---

## 12. Brand Voice

The tone should be:

- short
- confident
- witty
- sensory
- slightly unexpected

Avoid:

- corporate language
- aggressive wellness claims
- over-explaining ingredients
- motivational language
- internet slang that will quickly become outdated

## 14. Visual Identity Direction

The identity should balance two contrasting worlds:

### Botanical

- Organic
- Sensory
- Natural
- Tactile
- Ingredient-driven

### Contemporary

- Graphic
- Bold
- Structured
- Editorial
- Unexpected

The visual system should avoid looking like a traditional organic food brand.

---

## 15. Logo Direction

Develop a distinctive wordmark for **NUVRA**.

The wordmark should feel:

- contemporary
- recognizable
- slightly unconventional
- confident at large scale
- clear at small packaging sizes

Explore opportunities for a customized letterform or typographic detail that could eventually become a recognizable brand asset.

Do not rely entirely on a separate symbol.

The wordmark itself should carry personality.

---

## 16. Typography

Consider a two-typeface system.

### Display Typeface

Expressive serif, experimental grotesk, or characterful display typography.

Used for:

- headlines
- campaigns
- packaging statements
- social graphics

### Supporting Typeface

Neutral sans serif.

Used for:

- ingredients
- product information
- website navigation
- captions
- functional communication

The contrast between expressive and functional typography should become part of the identity.

---

## 17. Colour System

Avoid creating one generic corporate colour palette.

Instead develop a strong **master brand colour** combined with flavour-specific palettes.

### Citrus Basil

- Acid yellow
- Deep green
- Cream

### Peach Ginger

- Warm peach
- Burnt orange
- Soft beige

### Berry Hibiscus

- Deep berry
- Electric pink
- Muted lavender

The flavour colours should be distinctive enough that consumers can recognize variants instantly.

---

## 18. Graphic System

Develop a graphic system inspired by the collision of ingredients.

Possible directions include:

- abstract ingredient forms
- oversized typography
- collage
- distorted botanical illustrations
- fluid shapes
- unexpected cropping
- graphic flavour symbols
- experimental compositions

For example, a basil leaf does not need to appear literally.

Its shape, texture, structure, or movement could inspire an abstract graphic element instead.

---

## 19. Illustration Direction

Create a custom illustration language that represents ingredients without becoming traditional botanical illustration.

Possible characteristics:

- rough
- abstract
- oversized
- geometric
- collage-like
- screen-printed
- imperfect

Illustrations should provide contrast against clean typography.

---

## 20. Photography Direction

Photography should feel social and tactile rather than commercial.

Show:

- condensation
- ice
- hands
- tables
- food
- sunlight
- parties
- cafés
- unexpected compositions
- close-up ingredients

Avoid sterile studio images showing only a can against a plain background.

The viewer should imagine the product existing in their lifestyle.

---

## 21. Packaging System

Packaging is the hero application.

Create packaging for all three flavours.

Each flavour should feel unique while clearly belonging to the same family.

The can should contain:

- NUVRA logo
- flavour name
- botanical ingredients
- sparkling drink descriptor
- volume
- required product information

Consider making flavour names large enough to become part of the visual identity.

## 25. Competitive Landscape

For the project, research brands from categories such as:

- sparkling water
- kombucha
- craft soda
- functional drinks
- non-alcoholic beverages

Do not simply imitate their visual language.

Your competitor analysis should identify where visual conventions have become repetitive.

For example:

- minimal white packaging
- pastel palettes
- generic botanical illustrations
- heavy wellness language
- clinical ingredient communication

Then explain how NUVRA deliberately differentiates itself.

## 28. Success Criteria

The identity should successfully answer five questions.

### 1. Is it recognizable?
Could somebody identify a NUVRA product from several metres away?

### 2. Is it distinctive?
Would the packaging look noticeably different beside competing drinks?

### 3. Is it flexible?
Can the identity accommodate ten future flavours?

### 4. Is it appropriate?
Does it appeal to the intended audience without appearing childish or excessively luxurious?

### 5. Is it memorable?
Is there at least one strong visual device that somebody could associate specifically with NUVRA?

## 29. Portfolio Case Study Structure

### Chapter 01 — Introduction

Introduce NUVRA in one sentence.

Example:

> NUVRA is a botanical sparkling drink created for people looking for something more interesting than traditional soda.

Show one strong hero image.

### Chapter 02 — The Challenge

Explain the market problem.

Traditional sodas feel mass-market.

Wellness beverages often feel overly serious.

NUVRA needed to occupy the space between the two.

Show competitor/category observations visually.

### Chapter 03 — Strategy

Present:

- audience
- positioning
- brand idea
- personality

Keep this concise.

### Chapter 04 — Creative Concept

Introduce:

**Refreshment with character.**

Explain how unexpected ingredient combinations inspired an equally unexpected visual system.

Show moodboards and early explorations.

### Chapter 05 — Logo

Show:

- construction
- typographic decisions
- variations
- small-scale usage

Do not manufacture complicated geometry simply to make the logo appear sophisticated.

Only show construction when it genuinely explains the design.

### Chapter 06 — Visual System

Present:

- typography
- colour
- graphics
- illustration
- photography

Show how these elements interact rather than presenting them only as isolated brand-guideline pages.

### Chapter 07 — Packaging

Make this the largest section.

Show:

- all three cans together
- individual flavours
- close-ups
- physical environments
- multipack
- retail shelf

### Chapter 08 — Brand in Motion

Show applications such as:

- social media
- website
- campaign posters
- restaurant environment
- events

If possible, create simple animation showing typography, flavour transitions, or packaging.

### Chapter 09 — Final Brand World

Finish with several strong lifestyle applications that make the identity feel like a real brand rather than a student exercise.

The final image should be one of the strongest pieces in the entire project.

## 30. Project Goal for the Designer

The purpose of this project is not simply to create an attractive beverage can.

Your challenge is to demonstrate that you can build a **coherent brand system**.

A recruiter or potential client viewing the project should be able to see your ability to handle:

- strategy
- concept development
- typography
- logo design
- colour
- packaging
- art direction
- campaign design
- digital design
- brand consistency

The finished project should feel capable of existing outside Behance or a design portfolio.

It should look like a brand somebody could encounter in the real world.

---

## Portfolio Development Note

Spend roughly **30% of the project on the logo and 70% on the wider brand system**.

A strong portfolio case study should prove that the identity works across packaging, campaigns, digital interfaces, retail, and real environments—not just as a logo.
